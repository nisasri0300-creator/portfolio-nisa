Project 04 · Active Project

Supply Chain & Inventory Optimization

Strategic Supply Chain Framework | Integrated planning from demand to inventory, material, production, and purchasing.

01 · Objective

Build an integrated supply chain planning framework that connects demand with material requirements, inventory, production, and purchasing decisions. The objective is to maintain product availability while controlling excess inventory, stockout risk, working capital, and operational cost[cite: 10].

02 · Owner Perspective

From an owner perspective, inventory is not simply a quantity to monitor. It is capital that must continuously support sales and operations. The key question is not only "How much stock do we have?" but whether the right stock is available at the right time, in the right quantity, and at an acceptable cost[cite: 10].

  • • What demand is expected in the coming period?[cite: 10]
  • • Which products and materials require priority?[cite: 10]
  • • When will each material be needed?[cite: 10]
  • • How much should be purchased or replenished?[cite: 10]
  • • Which items are approaching stockout?[cite: 10]
  • • Which items are becoming excess or slow-moving?[cite: 10]
  • • How much capital is currently tied up in inventory?[cite: 10]
  • • Does the production plan match actual demand?[cite: 10]
  • • Can purchasing decisions be made from calculated requirements rather than assumptions?[cite: 10]

03 · Supply Chain Business Loop

DEMAND → PLANNING → MATERIAL → INVENTORY → PRODUCTION → AVAILABILITY → COST

The framework creates a continuous planning loop. Demand becomes the starting point; inventory and material requirements translate that demand into operational decisions; production and purchasing then return availability to the business[cite: 10].

04 · Planning Framework

Demand Planning

Historical Sales → Demand Pattern → Forecast → Future Demand

Understand expected demand before committing inventory and purchasing resources[cite: 10].

Production Planning

Demand → MPS → Production Requirement

Translate expected demand into planned production quantity and timing[cite: 10].

Material Planning

MPS → BOM → MRP → Gross Requirement → Net Requirement

Calculate what materials are actually required after considering existing inventory and planned supply[cite: 10].

Inventory Optimization

Stock → Safety Stock → Stock Coverage → Replenishment Requirement

Balance availability against excess stock and inventory carrying pressure[cite: 10].

Purchasing Planning

Material Shortage → Purchase Requirement → Supplier → Lead Time → Purchase Schedule

Determine what needs to be purchased, when it is required, and how supplier lead time affects timing[cite: 10].

Production & Fulfillment

Material Availability → Production → Finished Goods → Order Fulfillment

Ensure material readiness supports production and finished goods availability[cite: 10].

05 · Core Planning Logic

Planning Area Core Calculation / Logic Decision Output
Demand Historical demand + forecast assumptions Expected demand
MPS Demand − available finished goods Planned production
BOM Production quantity × material usage Gross material requirement
MRP Gross requirement − available / scheduled supply Net material requirement
Inventory On-hand + incoming − required stock Available / shortage / excess
Purchasing Net requirement + lead time Purchase quantity & timing

06 · Inventory Strategy

Inventory should be segmented according to its role and risk instead of being managed as one total number[cite: 10].

  • • Healthy Stock — sufficient to support expected demand[cite: 10].
  • • Safety Stock — protection against demand or supply uncertainty[cite: 10].
  • • Low Stock — approaching replenishment threshold[cite: 10].
  • • Stockout Risk — projected demand cannot be fulfilled from available supply[cite: 10].
  • • Excess Stock — inventory materially exceeds expected requirements[cite: 10].
  • • Slow-Moving Stock — inventory with weak movement and increasing capital exposure[cite: 10].

09 · Definition of Success

A mature supply chain should make inventory decisions more predictable and measurable: demand is visible, production is planned, material requirements are calculated, purchasing is timed, stockout risks are identified early, and excess inventory is actively controlled[cite: 10].
